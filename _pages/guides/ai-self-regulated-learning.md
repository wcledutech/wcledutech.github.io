<h2 id="english-title">English</h2>

The research question is how AI support can contribute to learners' ability to plan, monitor and evaluate their own work across learning tasks. A useful reading route connects evidence at three levels: patterns across interventions, the design of a learning environment, and the capabilities learners bring to AI use. Reading the studies together suggests treating the learning phase, the support offered and the outcome measured as separate design decisions. This makes the question more precise than asking whether an AI tool improves learning in general. The proposed connection between these levels is a synthesis developed in this guide. <!-- SRL01 -->

[Xu and colleagues' meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.70058) provides the starting point. It synthesizes 35 empirical studies published from 2013 to 2025 within Zimmerman's cyclical model and reports an overall effect of g = 0.507. The reported effects differ across forethought, task performance and reflection, and across measurement approaches. This contribution is useful when motivating a study that locates AI support within a particular learning phase or compares different kinds of outcome evidence. The synthesis also directs attention to whether improvements during supported activity persist as independent self-regulatory competence. This is an assessment question that can structure the next empirical study. <!-- SRL02 -->

That question can be translated into a concrete study plan. A researcher could examine how learners formulate a goal before consulting an agent, revise a strategy during a task and explain the changes after completing it. Immediate task scores, process records and a later task completed independently would answer complementary questions. These are proposed design choices derived from the meta-analysis's emphasis on phases and competence. For example, a successful revision during assisted work can be examined alongside the learner's ability to explain the relevant strategy and apply it in a new problem. The design connects the availability of support with evidence of what the learner can subsequently do. <!-- SRL03 -->

[MetaClassroom](https://doi.org/10.1177/07356331251322470) adds a perspective on the organization of the learning environment. This quasi-experimental study compares an immersive programming environment designed around three-dimensional learning progression with traditional classrooms. It reports improvements in programming achievement and self-regulated learning, including metacognitive skills, persistence and help-seeking, alongside learning beliefs and motivation. Its contribution to this topic is the joint consideration of disciplinary learning and regulation within an instructional design. It can be cited when explaining why a programming intervention evaluates multiple learning dimensions or integrates teaching, learning and assessment. The intervention studied is MetaClassroom; its findings concern that implemented environment. <!-- SRL04 -->

Learner readiness enters through [Jing and colleagues' ChatGPT programming study](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-024-02751-w). Using a quasi-experimental design with Python graphing tasks, it connects effectiveness in ChatGPT-assisted problem solving with AI literacy, programming knowledge and understanding of ChatGPT. Its contribution is to identify learner characteristics that matter within a concrete task setting. Read alongside MetaClassroom, it suggests a research design that describes both the environment and the learner's starting point. A proposed follow-up could examine whether the same regulatory prompt is useful to students with different programming foundations, with those foundations measured before the learning activity. <!-- SRL05 -->

[Liu and colleagues' review of GenAI agents](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2672562) offers a vocabulary for describing the support itself. Its synthesis distinguishes cognitive-epistemic, self-regulatory, affective and human–AI interaction roles. These categories help a researcher state what an agent is intended to contribute during learning. In a proposed programming activity, an agent could ask a learner to explain a debugging plan, review progress or evaluate a completed solution. Assigning such functions explicitly would make the intervention easier to describe and compare. The review supports the role classification; the example activities here are design proposals developed from that classification. <!-- SRL06 -->

These papers support a connected reading strategy. Use the meta-analysis to establish why phases and measurement matter; use MetaClassroom to examine the integration of disciplinary and regulatory outcomes; use the ChatGPT study to motivate attention to learner readiness; and use the agent review to specify educational roles. Together, they motivate a question that can guide a focused project: which form of agent support, introduced at which learning phase, helps learners with a specified starting level develop strategies they can use independently? A study organized around this question can define its intervention, comparison and assessment before selecting the technology. The relationship proposed here is a research agenda, with each paper supplying a distinct part of its rationale. <!-- SRL07 -->

<h2 id="chinese-title">中文导读</h2>

本专题关注一个具体问题：AI支持如何帮助学习者形成能够用于不同任务的计划、监控与评价能力？可以从跨研究证据、学习环境设计和学习者基础三个层次进入。综合这些研究，学习阶段、支持方式与评价指标应当分别界定，再说明它们如何连接。这一阅读思路把笼统的“AI能否促进学习”转化为可以实施的研究问题；三个层次之间的联系是本导读提出的综合框架。 <!-- SRL01 -->

[Xu等的元分析](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.70058)是理解这一问题的起点。研究按照Zimmerman循环模型综合2013—2025年的35项实证研究，报告总体效应量g = 0.507，并比较不同自我调节阶段和测量方式的结果。引用这篇论文，可以为聚焦某一学习阶段或采用多种评价证据提供依据。它进一步提出，评价AI支持需要关注学习者离开即时帮助后能够保留和迁移的自我调节能力。 <!-- SRL02 -->

这一评价问题可以转化为研究设计：观察学习者使用智能体前如何确定目标、任务中如何调整策略、完成后如何解释调整原因，再结合即时成绩、过程记录和后续独立任务。这些是依据元分析提出的设计建议。例如，研究可以同时检验学生能否在帮助下修改方案、解释相关策略，以及在新问题中独立运用策略，从而连接支持过程与学习者后续的独立表现。 <!-- SRL03 -->

[MetaClassroom研究](https://doi.org/10.1177/07356331251322470)把视角推进到学习环境。它采用准实验设计，将基于三维学习进阶的沉浸式编程环境与传统课堂比较，报告编程成绩、自我调节学习、学习信念和动机的改善，自我调节维度涉及元认知、坚持和求助。其贡献在于把学科学习与调节能力放入同一教学设计。讨论编程干预的多维评价或教学、学习与评价整合时，可以引用这项研究。 <!-- SRL04 -->

[Jing等的ChatGPT编程研究](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-024-02751-w)补充了学习者基础。研究以Python绘图任务开展准实验，分析AI素养、编程知识和对ChatGPT的认知与问题解决效果的关系。与MetaClassroom结合阅读，可以同时关注环境怎样组织学习，以及学习者带着什么基础进入环境。由此可提出后续问题：同一种调节提示对不同编程基础的学生是否同样有效？研究可在活动前测量基础，再追踪提示的作用。 <!-- SRL05 -->

[Liu等的生成式AI智能体综述](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2672562)提供了描述支持功能的分类，包括认知与知识、自我调节、情感以及人机交互角色。研究者可以据此明确智能体参与学习的任务。例如，在拟设计的编程活动中，智能体可以提示学生解释调试计划、检查进度或评价方案。综述为角色分类提供依据，这些具体活动则是本导读据此提出的设计示例。 <!-- SRL06 -->

阅读时，可以用元分析确定学习阶段与测量问题，用MetaClassroom理解学科与调节目标的整合，用ChatGPT研究引入学习者基础，再用智能体综述界定支持角色。这条路径形成一个可操作的问题：在特定学习阶段，哪种智能体支持能够帮助具有明确起点的学习者形成可独立使用的策略？围绕它，可以先确定干预、比较条件与评价任务，再选择技术。各篇论文分别提供研究依据，连接它们的关系构成后续研究议程。 <!-- SRL07 -->
